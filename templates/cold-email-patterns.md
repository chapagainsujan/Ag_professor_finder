# Cold email patterns

Choose the pattern that fits. Fill each slot with a fact from the dossier or the student profile. If a slot has no fact, drop the slot. Do not paste these lines as they are. They are shapes, not boilerplate.

## Pattern A: Prospective MSc or PhD student

```
Subject: Prospective [MSc/PhD] student, [topic] (start [term year])

Dear Professor [Lastname],

I'm [name], [degree] in [field] at [institution], [country]. I'm planning to apply for [MSc/PhD] in [term year] and wanted to ask whether you expect to take a student in [topic].

I read your [year] paper "[short title]" in [journal]. [One plain sentence on what the student took from it or a real question it raised, e.g. the method, the result, the data.] My thesis looked at [thesis topic], where I [specific task: ran, measured, analysed]. [One sentence that links this to the professor's work.]

[One honest line about a gap or a skill: e.g. I have used R for the field-trial data but not for genomics.] My CV is attached. Would you be open to a short call, or can you tell me how you prefer to receive applications?

Best regards,
[Name]
[Institution], [email]
```

## Pattern B: Fit with a funded project

Use only when the project is confirmed and public.

```
Subject: Question about the [project name] project and a [MSc/PhD] position

Dear Dr. [Lastname],

I'm [name], [one clause on background]. I found the [funder] project on [topic] on [source: the lab website, NIFA/GRDC/NSERC database] and wanted to ask whether the team expects to recruit graduate students for it.

[Two sentences connecting a specific skill or experience of the student to a task in that project.]

[One sentence on timing and what the student can bring or needs.] I've attached my CV. Thank you for your time.

Best regards,
[Name]
```

## Pattern C: Research assistant or visiting researcher

```
Subject: Visiting researcher enquiry, [topic], [dates]

Dear Professor [Lastname],

I'm a [status] at [institution] working on [topic]. I'm looking for a [duration] research placement from [month] to [month] and your group's work on [specific topic] is close to what I do.

[One sentence on a recent paper read and the question it raised.] [One sentence on the student's relevant skill.]

[Funding line, honest: "I have/do not have funding for this period."] My CV is attached.

Best regards,
[Name]
```

## Follow-up (10 to 14 days later, once)

```
Subject: Re: [original subject]

Dear Professor [Lastname],

Following up on my email from [date] in case it got buried. I'm still interested in [topic] and happy to send anything that helps, such as a transcript or my thesis summary.

Best regards,
[Name]
```

## Reply to a "yes, tell me more"

Answer exactly what was asked. Offer two times for a call. Send the requested documents. No new essay.

## Sample (fictional names, shows tone and length)

The professor, paper and numbers here are made up to show the style. Do not reuse.

```
Subject: Prospective MSc student, wheat scab resistance (fall 2027)

Dear Professor Alder,

I'm Maya Rai, a BSc Agriculture graduate from Tribhuvan University in Nepal. I'm planning to apply for an MSc in fall 2027 and wanted to ask whether you expect to take a student working on Fusarium head blight.

I read your 2025 paper on DON accumulation in spring wheat lines. The part I kept coming back to was that resistance and toxin levels didn't track each other in the field trial, which is something I saw on a smaller scale in my thesis, where I scored FHB severity on 24 local wheat lines across two seasons. I scored and entered all of the data and ran the ANOVA in R.

I haven't done any genotyping, so that would be new for me. My CV is attached. Would you have time for a short call, or is there a way you prefer to receive applications?

Best regards,
Maya Rai
Tribhuvan University, maya.rai@example.com
```

Why it works: one paper the student read, one real link to their own work, one skill gap stated, one ask.
