# Country notes

Short orientation, written from general knowledge. Admissions rules change by university and year. Tell the student to confirm on each program's official page before acting, and say so.

## United States

- PhD programs in agriculture sciences often admit students into a department with a specific advisor already agreed. Contacting the professor before applying is normal and often expected.
- Funding is usually a research assistantship (RA) paid from the advisor's grants, or a teaching assistantship (TA) from the department. A professor with active federal or commodity funding is more likely to have an RA.
- MSc thesis programs frequently carry an assistantship. Non-thesis MSc programs usually do not.
- Application deadlines are often December to January for fall start. Some departments admit year-round.
- Some professors list admission requirements such as GRE or English scores; many have dropped the GRE. Copy what the page says.
- Visa: F-1 student visa. Do not give legal advice. Point to the university's international office.

## Canada

- Thesis-based MSc and PhD programs generally need a faculty member who agrees to supervise before you are admitted. Emailing first is standard.
- Funding is usually a stipend package from the supervisor's grants plus university and scholarship money. NSERC and provincial or industry funds often support these.
- Application windows vary: many universities take applications from autumn for the following September start. Check each one.
- Some provinces and industry bodies offer graduate scholarships (for example in Saskatchewan, Alberta, Manitoba, Ontario). Search by province.
- Visa: study permit. Direct to the university's international office.

## Australia

- Higher degree by research (HDR: PhD, Master of Philosophy) applications generally need a confirmed principal supervisor. Universities ask for an expression of interest or supervisor agreement. Contact the professor first.
- Scholarships: the Research Training Program (RTP) is the main one. Industry research and development corporations (GRDC, Hort Innovation, MLA, Dairy Australia and others) and university-specific scholarships also exist. Scholarship rounds have fixed closing dates, often well before the start date, so check early.
- Domestic and international fee and scholarship rules differ. Ask the student which category they are in.
- Visa: student visa (subclass 500). Direct to the university's international office.

## Common to all three

- A professor will be more likely to reply if the email shows the student has read their recent work.
- Mention the intended start term and the degree level in the first lines.
- Attach a short CV (one to two pages). Include transcript only if asked.
- Follow up once after 10 to 14 days. Then move on.
- Check spam and the admissions portal as well as email.
